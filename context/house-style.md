# House style — how BD Partners writes and decides

*Last updated: 2026-09-22. Internal shared knowledge. Applies to every deliverable, memo and analysis produced in this workspace.*

## Deliverables

* **Tachles, numbers first.** A 60-second read at the top: the recommendation, the headline numbers, the next step. Detail below it, not before it.
* **Two artifacts, every time.** A client-facing document (confident, clean, decisive) and an internal companion note (honest confidence, what is soft, what to verify, the odds). The second is never shipped.
* **A verdict, not a menu.** When a decision is live, the output is a recommendation with its reasoning, not a balanced list of options. "It depends" is an unfinished answer.
* **The recommendation is the product.** A research dump without a decision is what the client already paid a consultancy for.
* **Length is a leak.** Pre-sale artifacts stay short by design: a point of view of 1–2 pages, a teaser of no more than five slides, an email of about 200 words.

## Evidence rules

* **Every factual claim carries a source and a date.** No exceptions, including in one-pagers and internal notes.
* **Hard inputs are separated from estimates**, and estimates are labelled on the face of the document.
* **Unknown scores zero and is flagged.** Never round an assumption upward to complete a row.
* **Verify entity identity and ownership** against a primary source; funding and ownership tables go stale.
* **A warm path is a real prior conversation, shared work history or a live thread.** A connection on a social network is not a warm path and is never presented as one. Tools may surface hints to check, never relationships.
* **No fabricated doors, quotes or references.** An invented path costs the relationship on the first call.

## Scoring and analysis

* **Transparent, additive scoring only.** Every score shows its breakdown inline (`Fit 6 = segment2 + capacity1 + why-now2 + event1`). If the breakdown is not visible, the score is not done.
* **No black-box economics.** Base, bear and bull rather than a single number; the 3–5 assumptions that drive the result stated openly and sensitivity-tested.
* **Apply a 2–4× optimism correction** to economics, signal strength and assumed readiness — in the internal note, so the client document stays confident while the analysis stays honest.
* **Watch asymmetric evidence grading.** Evidence for services tends to be judged more harshly than evidence for products; correct for it before sequencing decisions.
* **A cold test is not a market test.** One unanswered approach is not evidence about a category.

## Decisions

* **Pre-registered pass/fail criteria.** Any test, tool or channel gets its success criteria and a date set in advance, so results are not rationalised afterwards.
* **Build on demand, not on spec.** Nothing gets built without a specific, immediate client or sales use. Skills and templates are extracted from a run that worked, not designed before the first run.
* **Push builds down the substrate ladder** before accepting them (see the `bdp-build-eval` skill).
* **Every deferral gets a trigger** — the event that brings it back.
* **Regulatory structure can flip a recommendation entirely.** Check supervised-entity and licensing exposure before recommending any acquisition or entry route.
* **Method and evidence stay structurally separate.** Showing what was found is a client asset; showing how the method works is the practice's moat.

## Language

Internal work happens in Hebrew or English, whichever is faster for the person writing. Skills, templates, schemas and anything market-facing are written in English, so they port to a client or market that does not read Hebrew. Client-facing material for Italy is produced in English and validated in Italian by a native speaker before it is sent.

## Files

Name files `\[client]\_\[topic]\_\[YYYY-MM-DD]`. The method library in the repository is the source of truth; project knowledge files are mirrors of it, not separate versions.

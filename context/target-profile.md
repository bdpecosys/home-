# Target profile — who we target, and how targets are screened

*Last updated: 2026-09-22. Internal shared knowledge. Two distinct universes: clients of BD Partners, and targets inside a client engagement.*

## A. BD Partners' own client profile (who we sell to)

**The pattern being reproduced:** an established company standing up a **new business unit** with revenue ambition and no BD muscle in seat.

* Parent organization of roughly 500+ employees; the new unit still pre-scale.
* Preferably headquartered in Israel and expanding internationally. Additional access in Singapore and Germany.
* Sectors: fintech, payments and financial services first; also cybersecurity and AI infrastructure.
* The buyer is the executive who owns the new unit's revenue: CEO, VP Global, CBO, head of the platform or business unit.

**Screening gates and signals**

* **Reachability is a hard gate, not a scoring column.** No named door and no opening line means the company is not on the list, regardless of fit.
* **Employment signals**, scored: business-side roles posted in the last 90 days; no VP BD or CBO in seat; a unit young enough that the function is still being defined.
* **Corporate-structure signals:** a new brand or platform registered as a separate entity or page; a new executive with a global or expansion mandate appearing in the last months.
* **Conference and event presence is weighted heavily** — it indicates both intent and an access route.
* **Why-now must be dated and named.** An undated signal is a hypothesis.

**Investor route:** map a fund's portfolio first and confirm at least three relevant portfolio companies plus a real mandate, and only then ask for introductions to specific companies.

## B. Targets inside a client engagement (who the client sells to)

Every engagement universe carries four roles, and they are screened differently:

* **Buyers** — organisations that could buy the client's product. Scored on segment fit, capacity to buy (budget, procurement route, tender history) and a dated why-now signal.
* **Channel partners** — systems integrators, consultancies, distributors, platform and core vendors, associations. Scored on **reach into the buyer segment** and on a concrete motive to cooperate. A channel with no stated reason to cooperate is a wish, not a target.
* **Competitors** — tracked for positioning, not approached.
* **Influencers** — regulators, industry bodies, event organisers, hubs. They shape access and sometimes control it.

In public-sector and financial-infrastructure markets, deals usually close through partners and existing procurement vehicles rather than direct sales. Screen the channel universe from day one, not after outreach stalls.

## Signal preferences (what counts as a why-now)

Ranked by how reliably they have predicted a real opening:

1. A dated regulatory or compliance deadline that forces a decision.
2. A published tender, procurement notice or budget allocation.
3. A core-system replacement or major infrastructure programme.
4. A new executive with the relevant mandate, in seat under \~12 months.
5. Participation in a relevant conference or event (signal plus access route).
6. Partnership, expansion or innovation announcements.

Each signal is stored with its source URL and the date observed. Unsourced signals do not enter the pipeline.
